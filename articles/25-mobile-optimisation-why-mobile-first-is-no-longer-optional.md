# Mobile Optimisation: Why Mobile-First Is No Longer Optional

Google switched to mobile-first indexing for all websites in October 2023. This means Google predominantly uses the mobile version of your site for indexing and ranking. If your mobile experience is an afterthought, your rankings are suffering — even for desktop searches.

For EU service businesses, **mobile first design** is especially critical. In Spain, Italy, and Portugal, mobile traffic exceeds 75%. Even in B2B-heavy markets like Germany, 60%+ of initial research happens on mobile devices. A poor mobile experience is not a minor inconvenience — it is a direct ranking and revenue killer.

> **TL;DR — Key Takeaways**
> - **Google uses your mobile site for indexing.** A poor mobile experience hurts all rankings.
> - **60–75% of EU service business traffic is mobile.** Varies by country and industry.
> - **Tap targets must be minimum 48×48px.** Anything smaller frustrates users and triggers usability errors.
> - **Mobile page speed is a ranking factor.** Target LCP under 2.5 seconds.
> - **Simplified forms and click-to-call** are the highest-impact mobile conversion elements.

## What Is Mobile-First Indexing?

**Mobile-first indexing** means Google primarily crawls and indexes the mobile version of your website. Before 2023, Google used the desktop version. This shift reflects user behaviour: globally, mobile accounts for approximately 60% of web traffic.

**What this means practically:**
- If your mobile site has less content than desktop, Google indexes less content
- If your mobile site is slower, your rankings suffer
- If your mobile UX is poor, engagement signals (bounce rate, time on site) degrade
- Hidden or collapsed content on mobile may be weighted differently

## Key Mobile Optimisation Elements

### 1. Responsive Design

Your site must adapt seamlessly to any screen size. Use CSS media queries, flexible grids, and responsive images. Test on real devices, not just browser emulators.

**Common failure:** Desktop layouts that simply shrink on mobile, creating unreadable text and unclickable elements.

### 2. Tap Targets (48×48px Minimum)

Google's mobile usability guidelines specify minimum tap target sizes of 48×48 pixels. Anything smaller leads to mis-taps and frustration.

**Common failure:** Navigation menus with 20px links, forms with tiny checkboxes, CTAs too small to tap reliably.

### 3. Page Speed on Mobile

Mobile users are less patient than desktop users. According to [Google's research](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com), 53% of mobile users abandon sites that take longer than 3 seconds to load.

**Mobile speed targets:**
- LCP (Largest Contentful Paint): under 2.5s
- INP (Interaction to Next Paint): under 200ms
- CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift): under 0.1

### 4. Simplified Mobile Forms

Every additional field reduces mobile form completion by 15–20%. For mobile, ask: Name, Email, Phone. That's it. Save qualification questions for the follow-up call.

**Mobile form best practices:**
- Use appropriate input types (tel for phone, email for email)
- Enable autofill
- Use single-column layouts
- Show progress indicators for multi-step forms
- Place labels above fields (not beside)

### 5. Click-to-Call

For service businesses, a tap-to-call button is the highest-conversion mobile element. 60% of mobile users have contacted a business directly from search results.

**Implementation:** Use `tel:` links and prominent call buttons in the header and hero section.

### 6. Mobile Navigation

Hamburger menus are standard, but consider sticky headers with key actions (Call, Book, Menu) always visible. Avoid multi-level dropdowns that are impossible to navigate on touchscreens.

### 7. Readable Text Without Zoom

Minimum 16px font size for body text. Anything smaller triggers zoom on iOS, breaking the user experience.

## Common Mobile UX Mistakes

| Mistake | Impact | Fix |
|---------|--------|-----|
| Desktop-only navigation | Unusable on mobile | Implement hamburger or bottom nav |
| Tiny form fields | Low completion rates | 48px height minimum, single column |
| Full-screen popups | Penalised by Google | Use banner-style or delayed popups |
| Unplayable videos | High bounce rate | Use HTML5, avoid Flash |
| Tables that don't scroll | Content unreadable | Horizontal scroll or card layout |
| No viewport meta tag | Desktop layout on mobile | Add `<meta name="viewport">` |
| Auto-playing audio/video | Immediate bounce | Never auto-play with sound |

## FAQ

### Is mobile-first indexing active for all websites?
Yes. Google completed the rollout in October 2023. All websites are now indexed mobile-first.

### Does mobile performance affect desktop rankings?
Yes. Since mobile-first indexing is the default, your mobile experience directly impacts your rankings across all devices.

### What is the most common mobile SEO mistake?
Slow page speed. Mobile networks are less reliable than desktop, and mobile users are less patient. Compressing images and deferring JavaScript are the highest-impact fixes.

### Should I have a separate mobile site?
No. Responsive design is Google's recommended approach. Separate mobile sites (m.example.com) create duplicate content issues and are harder to maintain.

### How do I test my site's mobile performance?
Use Google's Mobile-Friendly Test, PageSpeed Insights (mobile), Search Console's Mobile Usability report, and test on real devices.
