# How to Choose the Right Keywords for Your Service Business

Most service businesses never struggle with visibility because their website is poorly designed. They struggle because they target the wrong keywords — phrases that either attract tyre-kickers or pit them against competitors they cannot beat. Getting **keyword research service business** right is the difference between ranking for terms that generate enquiries and wasting months on terms that never convert.

This guide walks you through a practical, step-by-step keyword research framework built specifically for service-based companies. Whether you operate a plumbing firm in Munich, a consulting practice in Amsterdam, or an IT services company in Dublin, these principles will help you identify, evaluate, and prioritise keywords that drive qualified traffic and measurable revenue.

> **TL;DR — Key Takeaways**
> - **Start with 15–25 seed keywords** across service categories, then expand using tools and competitor data.
> - **Search intent trumps volume every time.** A keyword with 150 monthly searches from buyers beats a 10,000-volume term from researchers.
> - **Classify every keyword** as informational, commercial investigation, or transactional before targeting.
> - **Long-tail keywords convert 2-4x better** than broad terms and face less competition.
> - **Local modifiers** (city, region, "near me") dramatically improve conversion rates for geographically-constrained services.

## Step 1: Build Your Seed Keyword Foundation

Every effective **keyword research service business** process starts with seed keywords. Ask these four questions:

1. **What do you call your services?** — "Management consulting," "emergency plumbing," "corporate tax advice"
2. **What would a customer type if they did not know your jargon?** — "Help with business strategy," "water leak in kitchen"
3. **What problems do you solve?** — "Reduce operational costs," "fix boiler pressure," "comply with GDPR"
4. **What outcomes do clients achieve?** — "Improve cash flow," "pass safety inspection," "migrate to cloud servers"

Aim for 15–25 seed keywords. Do not filter at this stage.

## Step 2: Analyse Competitor Keywords

Competitor **keyword analysis** uncovers opportunities you would otherwise miss:

1. **Identify 5–10 direct competitors** — businesses your size serving your geography
2. **Extract their ranking keywords** using Ahrefs or SEMrush
3. **Filter for relevant, achievable terms** — remove branded and ultra-competitive keywords
4. **Prioritise keywords where competitors rank 4–15** — realistic overtaking opportunities

## Step 3: Map Search Intent to Service Categories

Every keyword falls into one of four intent categories:

| Intent Type | Example Keyword | Value | Action |
|-------------|----------------|-------|--------|
| Informational | "what does a tax consultant do" | Low | Exclude or use for remarketing |
| Navigational | "Deloitte tax services" | Low | Not worth targeting |
| Commercial Investigation | "best tax consultant for small business" | High | Bid moderately, strong landing page |
| Transactional | "hire tax consultant Berlin" | Very High | Bid aggressively, direct CTA |

For service businesses, the sweet spot lies in **commercial investigation** and **transactional** intent. Before targeting any keyword, manually search it and analyse the top 10 results.

## Step 4: Balance Search Volume Against Keyword Difficulty

| Domain Authority | Target Difficulty | Target Volume |
|-----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Below 20 | Under 20 | 50–500 |
| 20–40 | 20–40 | 200–2,000 |
| 40+ | 40–60 | 2,000+ |

For service businesses, **relevance beats volume**. A keyword with 150 monthly searches from buyers in your city outperforms a 10,000-volume term from researchers globally.

## Step 5: Target Long-Tail Keywords for Services

| Type | Example | Intent | Competition |
|------|---------|--------|-------------|
| Broad | "marketing agency" | Vague | Global |
| Long-tail | "B2B content marketing agency for manufacturers in Sweden" | Precise | Low |

Generate long-tail variations using Google autocomplete, "People also ask," and question-based modifiers.

## Step 6: Add Local Modifiers for Geographic Relevance

- **Service + City:** "penetration testing services London"
- **Service + Region:** "architectural firm Bavaria"
- **Service + Near Me:** "accountant near me"
- **Service + Country:** "EU trademark attorney Spain"

## Your Keyword Evaluation Framework

| Factor | Weight | Score (1–5) |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Business Relevance | × 3 | 1–5 |
| Search Intent | × 3 | 1–5 |
| Monthly Volume | × 1 | 1–5 |
| Keyword Difficulty | × 2 | 1–5 |
| Local/Geographic Fit | × 2 | 1–5 |
| **Maximum** | **55** | |

Prioritise keywords scoring 35+. Deprioritise anything under 20.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I revisit my keyword targets?
Quarterly. Search behaviour shifts, competitors adjust, and new opportunities emerge.

### What is the most common keyword research mistake?
Chasing high-volume, low-intent keywords. A plumbing firm ranking #1 for "plumbing" gets fewer qualified leads than one ranking for "emergency plumber Kreuzberg."

### Should I target keywords my competitors rank for?
Yes, selectively. Target keywords where competitors rank 4–15 with content you can meaningfully improve upon.

### How many keywords should a service business target?
Start with 20–30 priority keywords mapped to specific pages. Expand as you gain traction.

### Do local keywords really matter for national service businesses?
Yes. Even national firms benefit because "near me" and city-specific searches have higher commercial intent.
