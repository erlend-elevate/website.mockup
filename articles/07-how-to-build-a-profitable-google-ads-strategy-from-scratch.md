# How to Build a Profitable Google Ads Strategy from Scratch

Most service businesses that try Google Ads fail at the account setup stage. They create one campaign, dump in fifty keywords, write two ads, and watch their budget evaporate with nothing to show for it. The problem isn't the platform — it's the absence of a structured google ads strategy that connects every euro spent to a measurable outcome.

Building a profitable account from scratch requires a clear framework: account architecture, disciplined keyword research, focused ad copy, dedicated landing pages, accurate conversion tracking, and a systematic optimisation cycle. This guide walks through each step with specific numbers, a sample campaign structure for a consulting firm, and the common mistakes that waste budget at every stage. By the end, you will have a repeatable process for launching campaigns that generate qualified leads at a cost your margins can absorb.

> **TL;DR — Key Takeaways**
> - **Account structure comes first.** One campaign per service, tightly themed ad groups, and single-keyword ad groups (SKAGs) for high-volume terms prevent budget leakage.
> - **Keyword research should focus on commercial intent.** Use Google's Keyword Planner alongside competitor gap analysis — not guesswork.
> - **Your ad copy must match search intent.** Headlines that mirror the exact search term consistently outperform generic claims.
> - **Landing pages are part of the ad.** A page built around one offer converts 2-4x better than a generic homepage.
> - **Tracking must be live before you spend.** Without conversion tracking, you are optimising blind.
> - **Optimisation is a weekly discipline.** The first 30 days determine whether your account scales or stalls.

## What a Google Ads Strategy Actually Covers

A google ads strategy is the complete system that connects your business offer to people actively searching for it on Google. It spans six core components: account structure, keyword research, ad creation, landing page design, conversion tracking, and ongoing optimisation.

Each component feeds the next. Poor account structure makes keyword management impossible. Weak keyword selection sends irrelevant traffic. Generic ad copy kills click-through rates. Bad landing pages waste clicks. Missing tracking data makes optimisation impossible. And without optimisation, even a well-built account slowly declines as competition and costs shift.

The businesses we see succeed treat Google Ads as a managed system, not a set-and-forget channel. That mindset shift — from "running ads" to "operating a search acquisition system" — is what separates profitable accounts from budget drains. ## Step 1: Account Structure — The Foundation Everything Else Builds On

Google Ads accounts are organised in a three-level hierarchy: account → campaign → ad group. Most service businesses need multiple campaigns — one per core service or market — with tightly themed ad groups underneath.

**The recommended structure for a consulting firm looks like this:**

| Level | Name | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|
| Campaign | Management Consulting — Lead Gen | Separate budget control for core service |
| Campaign | Change Management — Lead Gen | Different service, different messaging |
| Campaign | Digital Transformation — Lead Gen | High-value service with distinct keywords |
| Ad Group | Management Consulting — General | Broad match modified terms |
| Ad Group | Management Consulting — Industry | Industry-specific queries |
| Ad Group | Management Consulting — Near Me | Local intent searches |
| Ad Group | Management Consulting — Cost/Pricing | Price-aware prospects |
| Keywords | 5-20 per ad group | Tightly themed around one concept |
| Ads | 3-4 responsive search ads per ad group | Test different angles |

This structure gives you four critical controls: separate budgets per service, tailored ad copy per theme, clear performance visibility, and the ability to pause or scale individual services without affecting the rest.

**Common pitfall:** Creating one campaign with one ad group and stuffing every keyword into it. This destroys quality score, inflates cost-per-click, and makes it impossible to tell which service is actually profitable.

## Step 2: Keyword Research — Intent Beats Volume

Keyword research for service businesses should prioritise commercial intent over search volume. A term with 50 monthly searches and clear purchase intent outperforms a 5,000-volume informational term every time.

**Use this two-source approach:**

1. **Google Keyword Planner** (free, inside Google Ads): Start with 5-10 seed terms describing your services. Filter by location (your target EU countries), language, and average monthly searches. Look for keywords with "medium" or "high" competition — this indicates commercial value.
2. **Competitor gap analysis**: Search your target keywords manually. Note which competitors appear consistently. Use tools like SEMrush or Ahrefs to see which keywords they bid on organically — many of those translate directly to paid search opportunities.

**Classify every keyword by intent:**

| Intent Type | Example Keyword | Value | Action |
|-------------|----------------|-------|--------|
| Informational | "what is management consulting" | Low | Exclude or use for remarketing |
| Commercial investigation | "best management consulting firm" | Medium | Bid moderately, strong landing page |
| Transactional | "management consulting firm near me" | High | Bid aggressively, direct CTA |
| Price-aware | "management consulting costs" | High | Address pricing on landing page |

**Common pitfall:** Bidding on broad informational keywords. They burn budget and rarely convert. Add informational terms as negative keywords or assign them to a separate "education" campaign with a much lower bid cap.

## Step 3: Ad Copy — Match the Search, Not Your Brochure

Responsive Search Ads (RSAs) are now the default format in Google Ads. You provide up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions; Google's machine learning assembles combinations based on predicted performance. Your job is to feed it high-quality, intent-matched components.

**Headline rules that consistently perform:**

1. Mirror the keyword in at least one headline. If someone searches "change management consulting Frankfurt," a headline reading "Change Management Consulting in Frankfurt" earns higher relevance scores and lower CPCs.
2. Include a specific value proposition or differentiator: "20+ Years Experience," "Free Initial Assessment," or "Results in 90 Days."
3. Add a call-to-action headline: "Book a Free Consultation," "Get a Quote Today," "Speak to Our Team."
4. Use pinning strategically. Pin your brand or key offer to position 1 if it must appear, but let Google test unpinned variations for the rest.

**Description rules:**

- Description 1 should expand the core promise: what you do, for whom, and what outcome to expect.
- Description 2 should reduce friction: mention free consultations, no-obligation calls, fast response times, or certifications.
- Include sitelink extensions, callout extensions, and call extensions at the campaign level. These increase ad real estate and click-through rate by 10-15% on average, according to Google data.

**Common pitfall:** Writing generic ad copy that could apply to any business in any industry. If your competitor could use your ad without changing a word, it is too vague.

## Step 4: Landing Pages — Where Clicks Become Revenue

The landing page is where your ad spend converts to leads. A homepage is almost never the right destination. Dedicated landing pages built around one specific offer consistently outperform generic destinations by 2-4x.

**Every landing page should include:**

1. **Headline match**: The headline should reflect the ad and search term that brought the visitor. If the ad promised "Free Change Management Assessment," the landing page must repeat that exact phrase above the fold.
2. **One clear CTA**: One primary action — typically a form submission or phone call. Multiple competing CTAs reduce conversion rates.
3. **Trust signals**: Client logos, case study snippets, certifications, team photos, or testimonials. These address the primary barrier for cold visitors: "Why should I trust this firm?"
4. **Mobile optimisation**: Over 60% of B2B searches in Europe happen on mobile devices. Forms must be short (3-5 fields), buttons thumb-sized, and phone numbers tappable.
5. **Fast load speed**: [Google's own research](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/marketing-strategies/search/mobile-page-speed-conversion-data/) shows that every one-second delay in mobile load time can reduce conversions by up to 20%. Aim for under 2.5 seconds on 4G.

**Common pitfall:** Sending traffic to a homepage with ten navigation options, a slider, and no clear next step. Every click that lands on a confusing page is budget wasted.

## Step 5: Conversion Tracking — Measure What Matters

Conversion tracking must be live before you spend your first euro. Without it, you cannot optimise, you cannot calculate return on ad spend, and you cannot make informed budget decisions.

**Set up these three conversion actions at minimum:**

1. **Primary conversion: Form submission** — Track the thank-you page or form submission event via Google Tag Manager. Mark this as your primary optimisation goal.
2. **Secondary conversion: Phone call** — Use Google forwarding numbers or track clicks on tel: links. Many service businesses, especially in legal and home services, generate 40-60% of leads via phone.
3. **Micro-conversion: Time on site or scroll depth** — These help Google's algorithm understand engagement quality when lead volume is still low.

Import conversion data back into Google Ads from your CRM if possible. This closed-loop reporting — connecting ad spend to actual closed revenue, not just form fills — is what separates amateur accounts from professional ones. Google's own documentation on [conversion tracking best practices](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095821) emphasises importing offline conversions as the gold standard for lead-generation accounts.

**Common pitfall:** Counting every button click as a conversion. Track only actions that represent genuine business value — otherwise your optimisation algorithm learns the wrong behaviour.

## Step 6: Budget Planning — Realistic Numbers for EU Service Businesses

Budget planning starts with target cost-per-acquisition (CPA), not with what you feel comfortable spending. Work backwards from your unit economics.

**Example: A management consulting firm targeting mid-market clients**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average client value | €25,000 |
| Target cost to acquire a client | €2,500 (10% of value) |
| Expected lead-to-client conversion rate | 10% |
| Maximum cost per lead | €250 |
| Expected click-to-lead conversion rate | 3% |
| Maximum cost per click | €7.50 |
| Required monthly leads | 12 |
| Required monthly budget | €3,000 |

In the EU, cost-per-click varies significantly by industry. Based on our campaign data across 50+ clients, typical ranges are:

- **Legal services**: €6-€22 CPC
- **Management consulting**: €4-€12 CPC
- **B2B professional services**: €3-€9 CPC
- **Home services (plumbing, electrical)**: €2-€8 CPC
- **Healthcare (private clinics)**: €3-€10 CPC

Start with a daily budget of €50-€100 for the first 2-3 weeks. This gives Google's algorithm enough daily data to learn without burning through budget before you have optimisation insights. Scale only after you have 10-15 conversions with a stable CPA below your target.

**Common pitfall:** Setting a €500/month budget across ten campaigns. That fragments data so thinly that no campaign gets enough conversions to optimise. Better to run two campaigns well than ten campaigns poorly.

## Step 7: The Optimisation Cycle — Your First 90 Days

The first 90 days determine the trajectory of your account. Here is the week-by-week discipline that produces profitable campaigns:

**Week 1-2: Foundation and learning**
- Verify conversion tracking is firing correctly
- Review search terms daily and add negative keywords
- Check that ads are serving and landing pages load properly
- Do not make major changes — let data accumulate

**Week 3-4: First optimisation pass**
- Pause keywords with 50+ clicks and zero conversions
- Add negative keywords for irrelevant search terms (expect 10-20% of spend to be waste at this stage)
- Adjust bids: increase on keywords with CPA below target, decrease on those above
- Write new ad variations for underperforming ad groups

**Month 2: Refinement**
- Analyse device performance; adjust mobile bid modifiers if mobile CPC converts poorly
- Review geographic data; exclude postcodes or regions with high spend and no conversions
- Test new landing page headlines based on highest-performing ad copy
- Evaluate ad schedule data; reduce bids during low-conversion hours if patterns emerge

**Month 3: Scaling decisions**
- Calculate true ROAS including lifetime value, not just first transaction
- Increase budget on campaigns with CPA at or below target by 15-20% weekly
- Pause campaigns that have not reached target CPA after meaningful spend
- Expand to Performance Max or remarketing only after Search is profitable

**Common pitfall:** Making changes too frequently. Google's algorithm needs 5-7 days to stabilise after significant edits. Constant tweaking resets the learning phase and produces erratic results.

## FAQ

**What is a google ads strategy?**
A google ads strategy is the complete system connecting your business offer to active searchers through structured campaigns, targeted keywords, relevant ad copy, optimised landing pages, accurate tracking, and continuous optimisation. It ensures every euro of ad spend contributes to measurable business outcomes rather than disappearing into untracked clicks.

**How much budget do I need to start Google Ads in Europe?**
For most service businesses, a starting monthly budget of €1,500-€3,000 is realistic. This allows 2-3 campaigns to generate enough daily data for optimisation. B2B services with higher CPCs may need €2,500+ to reach statistical significance. The key is concentration — one or two well-funded campaigns outperform five thinly stretched ones.

**How long does it take to see results from a new Google Ads account?**
Expect 4-8 weeks before your account reaches stable, predictable performance. The first two weeks are learning; weeks 3-4 bring the first optimisation insights; by week 8, a well-structured account should show consistent lead flow at or near target CPA. Budget, competition, and conversion rate all affect this timeline.

**Should I use automated bidding or manual CPC?**
Start with manual CPC or Maximise Clicks with a bid cap until you have 15-20 conversions. This gives you control while data accumulates. Once you have conversion history, switch to Target CPA or Maximise Conversions. Automated bidding works best when Google's algorithm has sufficient data — launching with it from day one often produces erratic results for new accounts.

**What is the biggest mistake service businesses make with Google Ads?**
Sending traffic to their homepage instead of dedicated landing pages. A homepage has dozens of exit points and no single focus. Dedicated landing pages built around one offer, with one clear call-to-action, consistently convert 2-4x better. This one change alone often transforms unprofitable accounts into profitable ones.
